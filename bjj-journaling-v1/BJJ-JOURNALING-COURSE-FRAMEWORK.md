# BJJ Journaling: 4-Week Intro Course

> **A repeating 4-week course to help BJJ students track training, measure progress, and accelerate improvement**

---

## Course Overview

### Purpose
This course teaches BJJ students how to systematically journal their training, track sparring performance, and document technique progress. The system is designed to be gym-agnostic and works with any BJJ curriculum.

### Format
- **Duration**: 4 weeks (repeating cycle)
- **Weekly Commitment**: 15-30 minutes journaling time
- **Delivery**: Printable worksheets + notebook templates

### Student Outcomes
By the end of this 4-week cycle, students will:
- [ ] Log every training session with key metrics
- [ ] Track sparring attempts and success rates
- [ ] Document techniques learned with competency levels
- [ ] Complete weekly and monthly reviews
- [ ] Identify patterns and areas for improvement

## 4-Week Course Structure

### Week 1: Foundation Setup

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand the journaling system
- Set up core worksheets
- Establish logging habit

**Activities:**
1. Choose your journaling medium:
   - Printed worksheets in a binder
   - Copy templates into a notebook
   - Digital (future web version)

2. Set up your journal:
   - Print/prepare Daily Training Logs (5-7 copies)
   - Print first Weekly Review
   - Print Monthly Habit Tracker
   - Choose and print one Competency Matrix (Beginner recommended for new journalers)

3. First session logging:
   - Complete your first Daily Training Log
   - Focus on: Date, class type, techniques drilled
   - Don't worry about perfect sparring tracking yet

**Week 1 Worksheets Needed:**
- 01 - Daily Training Log (×5)
- 02 - Weekly Review (×1)
- 04 - Monthly Habit Tracker (×1)
- 08 - Competency Matrix Beginner (×1)

---

### Week 2: Sparring Focus

**Learning Objectives:**
- Track sparring rounds effectively
- Understand attempt vs. success tracking
- Calculate basic success rates

**Activities:**
1. Add sparring tracking to daily logs
2. Print and use Sparring Log for detailed tracking
3. Learn tally mark system:
   - A = Attempted
   - S = Successfully landed
   - D = Defended against opponent

4. Track at minimum:
   - Submissions (attempted/landed/defended)
   - Sweeps (attempted/landed)
   - Passes (attempted/landed)

5. Calculate your first success rates:
   - Success Rate = Landed ÷ Attempted × 100

**Week 2 Worksheets Needed:**
- 01 - Daily Training Log (×5)
- 11 - Sparring Log (×1)

---

### Week 3: Technique Tracking Deep Dive

**Learning Objectives:**
- Document techniques systematically
- Use competency levels meaningfully
- Identify technique gaps

**Activities:**
1. Set up Position Trackers for 2-3 positions you train most
2. Add techniques to your Competency Matrix
3. For each technique, record:
   - Name
   - Position it's from
   - Current competency level
   - Key details that help you remember it

4. Review: What positions do you train most? Least?

**Week 3 Worksheets Needed:**
- 01 - Daily Training Log (×5)
- 05 - Position Tracker (×3)
- 06 - Technique Type Tracker (×2) [optional]

---

### Week 4: Review & Analysis

**Learning Objectives:**
- Complete first full review cycle
- Identify patterns and insights
- Set goals for next cycle

**Activities:**
1. Complete Weekly Review for Week 4
2. Complete Monthly Review (or 4-Week Review)
3. Complete Sparring Summary for the month
4. Analysis questions:
   - What techniques did you drill most?
   - What positions do you spend most time in during sparring?
   - What submissions do you land? Get caught by?
   - What's your sweep success rate? Pass rate?

5. Set goals for next 4-week cycle:
   - One position to focus on
   - One technique to level up
   - One weakness to address

**Week 4 Worksheets Needed:**
- 01 - Daily Training Log (×5)
- 02 - Weekly Review (×1)
- 03 - Monthly Review (×1)
- 12 - Sparring Summary (×1)

## Key Metrics to Track

### Volume Metrics
- Training sessions per week/month
- Total training minutes
- Sparring rounds per session
- Techniques drilled

### Performance Metrics
| Metric | Formula |
|--------|---------|
| Submission Success Rate | Subs Landed ÷ Subs Attempted × 100 |
| Sweep Success Rate | Sweeps Landed ÷ Sweeps Attempted × 100 |
| Pass Success Rate | Passes Landed ÷ Passes Attempted × 100 |
| Defense Rate | Defended ÷ (Defended + Caught) × 100 |

### Progress Metrics
- Techniques at each competency level
- Positions trained (frequency)
- Goals achieved per week/month

## Tips for Success

### Getting Started
1. **Start simple** - Just log date, techniques, and a few sparring notes
2. **Build gradually** - Add more detail as the habit forms
3. **Log immediately** - Fill out your log right after class while memory is fresh
4. **Review weekly** - The weekly review is where insights emerge

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
1. **Trying to track everything** - Start with essentials, add complexity later
2. **Skipping reviews** - The review is more valuable than the daily logging
3. **Not being honest** - Accurate data matters more than impressive numbers
4. **Overcomplicating technique names** - Use whatever helps you remember

### Signs You're Getting Value
- You notice patterns in your sparring
- You can identify your strongest/weakest positions
- You remember techniques better after logging them
- Your success rates improve over time
- You set and achieve specific goals

## Next Steps After 4 Weeks

After completing your first 4-week cycle:

1. **Review your data** - What patterns do you see?
2. **Adjust your system** - Drop what's not useful, add what's missing
3. **Set new goals** - Based on your insights
4. **Continue the cycle** - The system improves with consistency

Consider upgrading:
- Move to Intermediate Competency Matrix after 2-3 cycles
- Add more Position Trackers as your game expands
- Create technique chains based on sparring patterns
